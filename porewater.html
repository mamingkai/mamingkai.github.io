Trace elements in pore waters of clay-rich sediments

What is the question?

Clay-rich sediments and rocks are almost impermeable, so the thin films of water between their mineral layers (the pore water) change very slowly. What is dissolved in that water records how elements have moved, reacted and been retained over long times.

Why it matters

Clay formations act as natural barriers. They are studied as host rocks and barriers for the geological disposal of radioactive waste, and they hold clues about past environments. Knowing which trace elements, including radionuclide analogues, stay mobile and which get bound to minerals is central to judging how well such barriers retain waste over thousands of years.

How I approach it

I combine careful pore-water sampling and trace-element analysis with what I know about mineral surfaces, especially iron minerals and redox reactions, to understand what controls the dissolved concentrations of trace elements.

Where it connects

The same questions I asked about phosphate and iron minerals (what binds an element, and what releases it) carry over directly to trace elements in clay-rich settings.
