Phosphate and sulfide: who is who at your pH?

Whether phosphate sticks to iron minerals, and how sulfide attacks them, depends strongly on the protonation state of both. Slide the pH to see which species dominate. These are textbook equilibria (25 °C, zero ionic strength), not experimental data.

Phosphate (H3PO4, pKa 2.15 / 7.20 / 12.35)

Sulfide (H2S, pKa1 ≈ 7.0)

In most freshwater sediments (pH 6–8.5) phosphate sits at the H2PO4-/HPO42- boundary, while sulfide switches from mostly H2S to mostly HS- right around neutral pH. Small pH shifts therefore change surface charge, adsorption affinity and the reactivity of dissolved sulfide at the same time. This is one reason pH is such a strong control in my iron–phosphorus experiments.

Second sulfide pKa is poorly constrained and negligible below pH 12, so it is omitted.
