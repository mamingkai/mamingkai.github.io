Bacteria meet minerals: how hard do they stick?

The idea

In soils and sediments, microbes and minerals constantly touch. Whether a bacterium sticks to a mineral surface influences mineral dissolution and transformation, how nutrients and pollutants are held, and how organic carbon is stored.

The method

Atomic force microscopy (AFM) uses a tiny cantilever with a sharp tip. By attaching a bacterial cell to the tip and pressing it against a mineral surface, the force needed to pull it off can be measured directly, at the scale of single cells.

The system

I studied the adhesion of the soil bacterium Pseudomonas putida to goethite (α-FeOOH), one of the most common iron oxides in soils.

Why it is useful

Direct force measurements tell us how strongly and under which conditions microbes attach to iron minerals, which helps explain larger-scale processes that are hard to see in bulk experiments.
